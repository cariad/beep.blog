I was supposed to start a new development contract this week. I ended up withdrawing before my name hit the dotted line -- but not before I treated myself to some new stationery.

See, I start new contracts with new notebooks, to keep client-confidential stuff separate from my personal nonsense, so I picked up a beautiful Rhodia hardback in the company colour.


And this time, I decided to try a new pen too.

I love writing with pencils, but I had a hankering for some ink, so I nabbed a fountain pen. Actually, my first fountain pen since I sat my GCSEs nearly a hundred years ago.

As always, the best parts of buying something are the research and rabbit holes. And at the bottom of this particular rabbit hole, I landed on a Lamy[^lamy].

[^lamy]: [lamy.com](https://www.lamy.com/)

{{< image name="lamy-and-webnotebook" >}}

## Lamy

…but not my _first choice_ of Lamy.

My first choice, was the Lamy Safari 2024 Special Edition _Pink Cliff_. Holy cow, aren't those colours just gorgeous?

{{< image name="csm_LAMY-safari-pink-cliff-FH_97786579c7" >}}

Sadly, Amazon wouldn't ship one to my local locker, so I went for my second choice: the Lamy Safari 2020 Special Edition _Candy Mango_.

{{< image name="lamy-safari-2020-special-edition-candy-mango" >}}

It's yellow. _Just_ yellow. Made from the same Acrylonitrile Butadiene Styrene
as LEGO, but with a matte finish. Sure, it's a little utilitarian; but
playful with it. Damnit, I adore it!

I'll admit, I was disappointed it didn't arrive in the same funky box that YouTubers unboxed theirs out of--the dark grey one with the windows--but I'll chalk that up to maybe it being a limited run. Or I got unlucky. Or just YouTubers getting nice shit for free.

But it would've been nice if the box was at least consistent.

I ordered the pen with a medium nib--"Size M"--and the sticker on the box agrees, but the printing on the flap indicated a fine--"F"--nib.

{{< image name="lamy-inconsistent-size-box-sticker" >}}

(Incidentally, the Swiss and Euro prices on the sticker pretty much align with the UK. I paid £24.95, while the Swiss would pay £22.17 and £21.27 elsewhere in Europe.)

And inside the box, the sticker on the pen claimed a fine nib too.

{{< image name="lamy-inconsistent-size" >}}

Luckily, the nib itself _is_ a medium. Disaster averted, and so on.

{{< image name="lamy-medium-nib" >}}

So, has Amazon been swapping pens' boxes and caps around? I'd be more upset if I'd paid a premium; I think I'll save my rage for when I spent more than twenty quid.

The pen came with a cartridge nestled inside the body. The cardboard ring cap prevents the cartridge being pierced when the body's screwed on, so removing it then replacing the body is all it takes to start the ink flowing.

{{< image name="lamy-bundled-cartridge" >}}

But does it write on the Rhodia paper?

## Rhodia Webnotebook

I flippin' love Rhodia paper, and this here book's got 192 pages of 90 gsm ivory (they mean _white_, not _elephantidae_) Étival-Clairefontaine paper pressed from mostly sawmill waste and brushed to a fine finish that's allegedly perfect for fountain pen nibs.

_Allegedly._ I suppose it's time for a test, but… good lord, it pains me to break into a notebook without a project to give it purpose.

But fine. Okay. For _you_, I'll do a little writing test. But only in the back. And only if you promise never to mention it again.
